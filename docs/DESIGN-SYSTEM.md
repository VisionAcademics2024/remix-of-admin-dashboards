# Vision CRM — spatial design system

A visionOS-style treatment of the CRM: an environment behind everything, dark
translucent glass in front of it, and one motion curve shared by every surface.

Everything below is implemented. `src/styles.css` is the single source of truth
for tokens; the components in `src/components/vision/` are the only things that
should need to know how the material is built.

---

## 1. What you asked me to send you — short answers

### Backgrounds: yes, please send them

Four placeholders ship in `public/environments/`, generated by
`scripts/generate-environments.py`. They are abstract on purpose and are meant
to be replaced.

To swap in real photography:

1. Drop the file in `public/environments/` as `<name>.jpg`.
2. Add a 64×36 blurred twin as `<name>-thumb.jpg` (it is the first paint, so the
   frame is never empty). `scripts/generate-environments.py` shows the sizing.
3. Add an entry to `ENVIRONMENTS` in `src/components/vision/environment.tsx`.

Nothing else changes.

**What makes a good one here**, which is narrower than "a nice photo":

| | |
|---|---|
| Size | 2560×1440 or larger, JPEG, progressive, under ~400 KB |
| Tone | Mid-to-dark overall for the dark themes. Glass is a *white film* — over a bright image it turns milky and text loses contrast |
| Detail | Soft and out of focus. Anything sharp fights the text sitting on top of it |
| Composition | Light source off to one side, darker centre. The content sits centre-frame |
| Avoid | Hard horizons, faces, text, logos, high-frequency detail, strong greens (they clash with the success colour) |

Landscapes at dusk, long-lens skies, gradients on water, aerial dunes — all work.
Bright daylight scenes generally do not, unless you want another light theme, in
which case send it and I will pair it with dark type like `daylight`.

### SF Pro: already there on Apple devices, and I would not self-host it

`-apple-system` in the font stack **is** SF Pro on macOS, iPadOS and iOS. No
download, no files, no licence question. That covers most of how this will be
used, and it is already wired up.

Windows and Android fall through to Segoe UI Variable / Inter / system-ui, which
is close in proportion but not identical.

To close that gap you would have to self-host the `.woff2` files, and **that is
where I would stop and check with someone**. Apple's font licence covers using
SF for designing and developing software for Apple platforms; it does not grant
the right to embed the fonts in a website served to any device. A CRM in a
browser is not obviously covered. I am not the right party to make that call, so
I have not added the files.

The plumbing is ready either way: `src/styles.css` declares both faces as
variable fonts pointing at `/fonts/SF-Pro-Display.woff2` and
`/fonts/SF-Pro-Text.woff2`. Drop the files in and they take effect. Leave them
out and the browser skips the rules silently — the only trace is a 404 in the
console.

**If you want a licensed match on every platform instead:** Inter (SIL Open Font
License, free to embed) is the usual substitute and is already in the stack.
Say the word and I will self-host it so Windows matches Mac.

### Icons: nothing to attach

The project already uses **Lucide**, and it is a good choice here — geometric,
consistent stroke, and the weight is tuned to sit near SF Symbols (`1.7` at rest,
`2.1` when active).

**Do not send SF Symbols.** Same problem as the font, only more clear-cut: the
licence permits use in apps for Apple platforms and explicitly does not allow
the symbols to be embedded or redistributed elsewhere.

If Lucide ever feels wrong, **Phosphor** (MIT) is the closest free family to SF
Symbols and is a drop-in swap.

### Images inside the app

All placeholders, as you asked. There are no photographic assets in the
interface itself — student and tutor identity is carried by initials, monogram
tiles and tutor colours, which stay correct when nobody has uploaded a photo.

## 2. How the material works

Three ideas, in order of importance.

**There is a place behind the glass.** `<Environment />` paints a fixed image, a
scrim, and a very slow drift. This is not decoration: a translucent panel over a
flat colour reads as grey plastic. The tonal range behind is what makes the
material look like glass.

**Material is dark and translucent, never white.** visionOS glass is a
low-opacity white film over a dark scrim, blurred hard and saturated. The white
you see is the *specular edge*, not the fill.

```
.glass            fill: white 9%   blur 32px  saturate 180%
.glass--thin      fill: white 6%   blur 24px    chrome, chips
.glass--thick     fill: white 13%  blur 44px    ornament, dialogs, header
.glass--solid     fill: 72% dark   blur 48px    anything carrying rows
```

Every tier shares the same edge, shadow and radius, so they read as one
substance at different depths:

```
border            1px  var(--edge)
inner highlight   inset 0 1px 0 var(--edge-top)     ← the specular top edge
ambient shadow    0 2px 8px -4px  var(--shadow-ambient)
key shadow        0 24px 56px -24px var(--shadow-key)
```

**Everything has a z-position.** `.lift` raises a surface 2px and scales it
1.012 on hover, then presses to 0.988 on click. `.press` is the same idea for
controls that sit *in* a surface rather than on top of it.

### The one deliberate compromise

`glass--solid`. Dense tables over sheer glass look superb in a screenshot and
are miserable to read after ten minutes of marking a roll. So **chrome is sheer,
data is solid** — `TableShell` is nearly opaque while everything around it stays
translucent. This is the only place the illusion is traded away, and it is
traded on purpose.

## 3. Motion

One curve does almost everything:

```css
--ease-spatial: cubic-bezier(0.32, 0.72, 0, 1);   /* quick out, long soft settle */
--dur-fast: 180ms    presses, row hovers
--dur-base: 320ms    lifts, colour, the ornament widening
--dur-slow: 520ms    entrances, environment cross-fade
--dur-ambient: 24s   the environment drift
```

- **Entrances** (`spatial-in`) come from 14px back with a 6px blur that resolves
  as they settle — arriving from behind the viewer rather than sliding in.
- **Stagger**: `.stagger` delays each child by 45ms so a screen assembles
  front-to-back. Every one of the 14 screens uses it.
- **Specular**: `useSpecular()` writes `--mx/--my` from the pointer straight to
  the element's style — no React state, because this fires every frame and a
  re-render per frame would be pure cost for something purely visual.
- **Loading** is a light sweep (`.shimmer`), never a grey pulse.

`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` collapses all of it to ~0ms and disables the
hover lift. That is a blanket rule at the bottom of `styles.css`.

## 4. Navigation

`AppSidebar` is an **ornament**: a floating glass rail hung beside the window,
not a column inside it. Icons only at 68px; hovering widens it to 248px and
reveals labels and group headings. The active item gets a filled pill and a
glowing pip on its left edge.

Expanded, it covers live content, so it thickens to `--mat-solid` while open —
otherwise headings read straight through it.

The environment picker sits in the **header**, not the rail. visionOS keeps
environment control in Control Center rather than inside an app's own
navigation, and it also kept the rail from outgrowing a laptop screen.

## 5. Themes

Two, and the **environment chooses**. Picking Daylight switches to light type;
picking any of the other three switches to dark. A dark scene cannot carry dark
text, so letting them drift apart would only ever produce something unreadable.

Dark is the default and the one the system is designed around.

> **A bug worth remembering:** the dark block is scoped `:root.dark`, not
> `.dark`. Both match `<html>`, but at equal specificity to `:root` the winner
> is decided by source order alone — which silently made the whole dark theme
> unreachable the first time it was built. Do not loosen that selector.

## 7. Performance

`backdrop-filter` is the expensive part. Two rules keep it sane:

- Blur layers are bounded. Panels blur; rows, pills and cells inside them do not.
- Where `backdrop-filter` is unsupported, every glass class falls back to a
  solid fill rather than shipping unreadable transparent panels.

If a screen ever feels heavy, the first thing to try is dropping that screen's
panels from `regular` to `solid` — it removes a compositing layer per panel.
